# Fringe Field

*The visual language of the ElectroClimb repository, banner and posts.*

**Fringe Field** treats the invisible as the subject. What matters most in the work can't be photographed: a force that exists only at the boundary between two surfaces. The design gives that boundary a body. Fine lines curve out of a row of small, perfect marks and close on their neighbours, again and again, until repetition itself reads as presence. Nothing is illustrated; everything is measured.

Space is dark and quiet, the colour of graphite under a work lamp, and almost all of it is left alone. One object occupies it, lit from a single direction, placed off-centre so the emptiness on the other side carries weight. The object is never decorated or outlined. It sits in the field the way a specimen sits on a slide, and the margin around it is part of the specimen.

Colour is rationed with painstaking care. A single warm amber stands for energy and appears only where something is live. A single cool blue answers it as its opposite pole, and never touches it. Every other tone is a step of grey chosen by eye until the transitions disappear. If a third colour seems necessary, the composition is wrong, and the composition is changed.

Rhythm comes from instruments: tick marks, scales, alternating pairs, arcs nested at exact intervals. These are drawn with the discipline of an engraver, hairline-thin and perfectly even, the product of deep expertise and many patient refinements. They suggest that the scene has been surveyed, that someone stood here with a ruler and cared about the answer.

Type is the system's own voice, set in the platform's native sans with nothing added. One name is allowed to be large. Everything else is small, widely spaced and factual, placed like reference markers on a drawing, never overlapping, never crowding an edge. Words label; they do not explain.

The finished piece should look as though it took far longer than it shows: meticulously crafted, every alignment checked, every curve earned, the work of someone at the top of their field who removed things until only the necessary remained.
